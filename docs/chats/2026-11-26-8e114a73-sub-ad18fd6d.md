610	<section class="section reveal" id="background-and-scenario"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-buildings" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">Background and scenario</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="body-3 measure">A client runs its sales team in Salesforce, a customer relationship management (CRM) product. Tequity has built the client an operations portal. Finance and customer-success staff work in the portal.</p><p class="body-3 measure">The same people exist in both places, as customers and as contacts. A sales person creates a customer in Salesforce. The portal should show that customer. A finance person on the portal then corrects the billing city. Salesforce should show the new city. Later, a sales person changes the phone number in Salesforce. The portal should show the new phone, and the city the finance person set should still be there.</p><p class="body-3 measure">The client also needs the history. They need to know who changed the phone, and who changed the city. The fact of an earlier edit stays on record after a later edit.</p><p class="body-3 measure">Salesforce stores its own copy. The portal stores its copy in PostgreSQL (Postgres). The two copies have to stay in sync. We are designing and building our side. Salesforce already exists, and it stays as it is.</p></div></div></section>
611	<section class="section reveal" id="the-systems-involved"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-share-network" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">The systems involved</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="body-3 measure">Three parties matter.</p><ol class="cards cols-3 wide"><li><div class="body-3"><strong>Salesforce.</strong> The client's CRM. Sales users edit records here. Salesforce has its own database.</div></li><li><div class="body-3"><strong>The portal.</strong> Our application. Finance and customer-success users edit records here. Our database is Postgres.</div></li><li><div class="body-3"><strong>The network between them.</strong> Salesforce and the portal talk over the public internet, using Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure (HTTPS). Each side keeps its own database. HTTPS is the only path between them.</div></li></ol><p class="title-3 measure">Who calls whom:</p><ul class="list list-ul measure"><li><div>When a record changes in Salesforce, Salesforce calls us. The call is a webhook: an HTTPS request to an endpoint we host. The webhook names the record and describes the change.</div></li><li><div>When a record changes in the portal, we call Salesforce. The call uses Salesforce's official record application programming interface (API): the create and update operations Salesforce already documents. The portal reaches Salesforce through that record API.</div></li><li><div>A sales user talks only to Salesforce. A portal user talks only to the portal. Neither user talks to the other system.</div></li></ul><p class="body-3 measure">The rate limit below applies to the requests we send to Salesforce. Webhooks Salesforce sends to us sit outside that cap.</p><div class="frame frame-diagram measure"><pre><code> Sales user                         Finance / customer-success user
612	     |                                          |
613	     | uses                                     | uses
614	     v                                          v
615	 Salesforce CRM                           Tequity portal

A call to the Salesforce record API is a request we initiate. Salesforce may accept it, reject it, or be unreachable.</p></div></div></section>
630	<section class="section reveal" id="requirements-and-scale"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-list-checks" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">Requirements and scale</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="title-3 measure">Records:</p><ul class="list list-ul measure"><li><div>Customers and contacts. About these fields, and no others, for this exercise: customer name, status, and owner; contact full name, phone, email, city, owner, and the customer they belong to.</div></li><li><div><strong>5 million records</strong> in production, customers and contacts counted together.</div></li></ul><p class="title-3 measure">Sync:</p><ul class="list list-ul measure"><li><div>While both systems are up, a create or an update on either side is visible on the other side within <strong>30 seconds</strong>. Thirty seconds is the worst case. Everyday sync should land sooner.</div></li><li><div>Both sides may create. Both sides may update.</div></li><li><div>The current value of a field is the one we can justify. The earlier values, who made them, and when, stay on record. Dropping that history counts as losing a change.</div></li><li><div>An update we send to Salesforce must not bounce back and forth forever.</div></li></ul><p class="title-3 measure">Webhooks:</p><ul class="list list-ul measure"><li><div>Salesforce may deliver the same webhook twice.</div></li><li><div>Salesforce may deliver webhooks in a different order from the order of the edits.</div></li><li><div>Each webhook carries at least the fields in the sample below. <code>occurredAt</code> is the time Salesforce gives the event.</div></li></ul><p class="body-3 measure">The CRM API allows at most <strong>100 requests per second</strong>. That is our cap on calls to Salesforce. We choose how far under the cap to run.</p><p class="body-3 measure">We left the peak edit rate open. State the rate you assume, in edits per second, and use it in the arithmetic.</p><p class="body-3 measure">Sample webhook. This is enough to start. You may add fields. Say why.</p><div class="frame frame-json measure"><pre><code>{

647	<section class="section reveal" id="constraints"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-barricade" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">Constraints</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="body-3 measure"><strong>Rate limit.</strong> At most 100 requests per second to the Salesforce record API. The cap counts the calls we make, including retries. Webhooks arriving at our endpoint sit outside this cap. If Salesforce tells us to slow down, we slow down, and we keep the work.</p><p class="body-3 measure"><strong>Outages.</strong> Design for the Salesforce API being unreachable for <strong>6 hours</strong>. Webhooks Salesforce tried to send during that window may never arrive. Pending calls to Salesforce must still be there after our own process restarts.</p><p class="body-3 measure">Thirty seconds applies while both systems are up. Catch-up after an outage is a different target. Drain the backlog as fast as the rate limit allows. Compute how long that takes, in minutes, for the edit rate you assumed. Show the division.</p><p class="body-3 measure"><strong>Consistency.</strong> Two people may edit the same record at the same time, on the same side or on opposite sides. Edits to different fields of that record both have to survive. Edits to the same field need one current value and a kept history.</p><p class="body-3 measure">Say what you use to decide which edit is current, on a webhook and on a portal save. Name the field. If you need an API this note does not give you, mark it as an assumption.</p><p class="body-3 measure"><strong>Out of scope for this weekend:</strong> deletes, Salesforce login screens, a visual user interface, a second region, and a load test against real Salesforce. If you write a program, call a fake CRM you write yourself.</p></div></div></section>
648	<section class="section reveal" id="what-you-deliver"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-files" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">What you deliver</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="body-3 measure">Three things are required. A program is optional.</p><p class="tagline heading-3" data-tagline><span class="word">We</span> <span class="word">ask</span> <span class="word">for</span> <span class="word">the</span> <span class="word">chats</span> <span class="word">and</span> <span class="word">the</span> <span class="word">commits</span> <span class="word">because</span> <span class="word">we</span> <span class="word">want</span> <span class="word">to</span> <span class="word">see</span> <span class="word">how</span> <span class="word">you</span> <span class="word">prompt</span> <span class="word">the</span> <span class="word">artificial</span> <span class="word">intelligence</span> <span class="word">(AI)</span> <span class="word">assistant,</span> <span class="word">and</span> <span class="word">how</span> <span class="word">your</span> <span class="word">thinking</span> <span class="word">develops.</span> <span class="word">The</span> <span class="word">final</span> <span class="word">document</span> <span class="word">alone</span> <span class="word">does</span> <span class="word">not</span> <span class="word">show</span> <span class="word">that.</span></p><div class="panel measure"><div class="panel-body"><h3 class="heading-4">1. 215	
216	- The Dramatiq outbound task is a single pass over due rows. Redis wakes it, and a timer task
217	  every 5 s backs up a lost wake-up. **Dramatiq's own retries are off for this task**, so no
218	  backoff of hours and no dead-letter set.
219	- Workers claim due rows with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and set `lease_until`. If a worker
220	  dies mid-call, the lease runs out and another worker sends the row again.
221	- **We send the record's current value, not the edit.** The worker reads the current values of
222	  the changed fields at send time. So an older row can't overwrite a newer value in Salesforce,
223	  several pending edits to one record collapse into one call, and an update sent twice is
224	  harmless.
225	- **Creates carry our record ID as an external ID** and use upsert (assumption Q13). A create
226	  retried after a timeout then matches the existing record instead of making a duplicate. On
227	  success we store the Salesforce ID.
228	- Every call goes through the shared limiter at R = 90 req/s (Q10), retries included.
229	
230	Each answer from Salesforce:
231	
232	| Answer | Action |
233	|---|---|
234	| Accepted | `sent`. On a create, store the Salesforce ID. |
235	| Unreachable, timeout, 5xx | Back to `pending`. Retry at 1, 2, 4, 8 s…, capped at 60 s. Four quick retries take 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 = 15 s, inside the 30 s target. |
236	| 429 slow down | Back to `pending` at `Retry-After`. The shared limiter pauses until then, halves its rate, and climbs back by 5 req/s every 30 s to 90. |
237	| 400-type rejection | `needs_review` and an alert. Never dropped (Q9). A person fixes it and requeues. |
238	| 401 / 403 | Stop all sending and page someone. Our credentials are wrong, so every call would fail. |
239	
240	Salesforce is marked **down** after 5 consecutive unreachable failures. Sending stops and a probe
241	runs every 10 s. When the probe succeeds, catch-up runs, then every pending row is due at once
242	(§10).
243	
244	The outbox model is built (task 1.3.2). Not built yet: the outbound task, the limiter and the probe. The fields in an
245	outbound call are the fields the portal user changed, read at their current values.
246	`src/config/settings.py` already turns on Dramatiq's `Retries` middleware for every actor
247	(`max_retries` 20 by default). The outbound task must set `max_retries=0`.
248	
249	## 7. Rule: both sides change the same record
250	
251	Decided. Full mechanism: [Architecture](https://github.com/hellosrijan/sync-data/wiki/Architecture), `apply(change)`.
252	
253	- **Per field, the newest `occurred_at` wins.** On a webhook that's `occurredAt`. On a portal
254	  save it's our server clock when the save arrives (the portal sample has no time field), and
255	  the actor is the logged-in user (answers Q5).
256	- Each record stores the last-applied time and source of every field. A change is applied only
257	  if its `occurred_at` is newer than that field's stored time. Edits to different fields
258	  therefore both survive.
259	- **Every edit is written to the `field_change` history**, whether applied or superseded, inside
260	  the same transaction. It isn't a Django signal: signals miss superseded edits and bulk writes.
261	- Concurrent edits to one record are serialised with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`.
262	- **Exact tie: Salesforce wins.**
263	- **Two portal users, same field (Q4):** both saves apply, and the later one is current. There's no conflict check, so the earlier editor isn't warned. Both edits stay in history.
264	- *Assumption:* Salesforce's clock and ours are NTP-synced, with drift well below the gap between
265	  real edits. *If not:* a later real edit can be ranked as earlier and recorded as superseded.
266	
267	## 8. Rule: duplicate webhook, and an older webhook after a newer one
268	
269	Decided.
270	
271	- **Duplicate:** `change_event.event_id` is unique. The key is `eventId` for a webhook, a new
272	  UUID for a portal save, and `record_id` + Salesforce modified time for a catch-up result. A
273	  repeat hits the unique constraint, so we return OK and do nothing.
274	- **Dedupe retention: 7 days.** *Assumption:* Salesforce redelivers within 7 days. *If not:* a repeat
275	  that arrives after its ID has expired is not caught as a duplicate. The per-field time check still
276	  stops it from overwriting a newer value, but it adds a second history row.
277	- **Older after newer:** per field, an `occurred_at` older than the field's stored time is
278	  written to history as *superseded*. It never becomes the current value. The earlier edit stays
279	  on record, as the brief requires.
280	
281	## 9. Rule: stopping the sync loop
282	
283	Decided.
284	
285	- Only a **portal** change that applied at least one field creates an outbox row. Webhook and
286	  catch-up changes never do, so nothing received from Salesforce is ever sent back.
287	- **Our echo:** a webhook whose `changedBy` is our integration user is our own update coming
288	  back. It's recorded as a confirmation and never applied. This stops a delayed echo from
289	  overwriting a newer portal edit.
290	- *Assumption:* `changedBy` shows our integration user for writes made through the API. *If not:* our
291	  own echo is applied like any Salesforce change. It is still not sent back, because webhook changes
292	  never create outbox rows.
293	
294	## 10. The 6-hour outage
295	
296	Mechanism: [Architecture](https://github.com/hellosrijan/sync-data/wiki/Architecture), "After an
297	outage".
298	
299	What is on disk:
300	
301	| Item | Where | Note |
302	|---|---|---|
303	| Pending calls (portal edits not yet in Salesforce) | Postgres `outbox` | Committed in the same transaction as the edit. Survive any restart of ours. Never dropped (Q9). |
304	| Portal records and history | Postgres | Not affected by the outage. |
305	| Wake-up signal for the worker | Redis | Losing it loses no work; the outbox still holds every pending call. |
306	| Webhooks missed during the outage | Not on our disk | Recovered by the catch-up query (path C, assumption Q3). |
307	
308	How it drains:
309	
310	1. While Salesforce is down, outbound calls fail and their outbox rows stay pending. A probe
311	   checks every ~10 s whether Salesforce is back.
312	2. On recovery, catch-up runs first: it fetches what changed in Salesforce since the watermark and
313	   applies it. Same-field conflicts are settled before we send anything. Catch-up also runs every 5 min as a safety net (path C, Architecture).
314	3. Then the outbox drains at R = 90 req/s through the shared limiter. Several edits to one record
315	   are merged into one call.
316	
317	Retry policy (Q7, §6): the outbox drives retries at the limiter's pace, and no wait is longer
318	than 60 s. Dramatiq's backoff isn't used, so the drain figures below hold. Recovery is noticed
319	within 10 s (the probe). Catch-up then takes 54 calls, under 1 s, before the drain starts.
320	
321	Arithmetic. Symbols:
322	
323	- T = outage length = 6 h = 21,600 s.
324	- r = portal edits per second during the outage. **Assumed 5/s at peak (Q1).**
325	- R = run rate = **90 req/s (Q10)**, under the cap C = 100.
326	- B = backlog = r × T. One outbound call per edit (merging only makes it smaller).
327	
328	Worst case: the outage covers 6 hours of peak, and new edits keep arriving at r while we drain.
329	
330	- B = 5 × 21,600 = **108,000 calls**
331	- Catch-up first: 108,000 Salesforce-side changes ÷ 2,000 records per query = **54 calls**, < 1 s
332	- Drain: B ÷ (R − r) = 108,000 ÷ (90 − 5) = 108,000 ÷ 85 = **1,271 s ≈ 21.2 min**
333	
334	Other cases, same formula:
335	
336	| Case | r (portal edits/s) | Backlog B | Division | Drain |
337	|---|---|---|---|---|
338	| Worst case: 6 h of peak | 5 | 108,000 | 108,000 ÷ 85 | **21.2 min** |
339	| Normal working day | 1.75 | 37,800 | 37,800 ÷ 88.25 | **7.1 min** |
340	| Overnight (quiet hour) | 0.1 | 2,160 | 2,160 ÷ 89.9 | **24 s** |
341	
342	The r column is the portal share, half of the total in Q1. Working hours average 3.5 edits/s in
343	total, so 1.75 portal; the quiet hour is 0.2 in total, so 0.1 portal.
344	
345	Sensitivity: drain minutes = 21,600 · r ÷ (90 − r) ÷ 60 = 360 · r ÷ (90 − r).
346	
347	| r | Drain after 6 h at that rate |
348	|---|---|
349	| 5 (assumed) | 21 min |
350	| 10 | 45 min |
351	| 20 | 1.7 h |
352	| 45 | 6 h (as long as the outage) |
353	| ≥ 90 | never drains |
354	
355	The design has about 9× headroom over the assumed portal peak before draining takes as long as
356	the outage.
357	
358	Full reconcile, for comparison (Q3): 5,000,000 records ÷ 2,000 per query = 2,500 calls ÷ 90 req/s
359	≈ **28 s** of cap. Without a query API, one record per call: 5,000,000 ÷ 90 ≈ 15.4 h.
360	
361	## 11. Three numbers to watch
362	
363	| Metric | Page someone at |
364	|---|---|
365	| Portal edits per second (the rate that fills the outbox) | **30/s sustained for 5 min.** That's 6× the assumed peak (Q1); at 30/s a 6-hour backlog takes 3 h to drain, and at 45/s draining takes as long as the outage. |
366	| Age of the oldest `pending` outbox row, while Salesforce is up | **> 2 min.** The 30 s sync target is clearly broken. Excludes outages, when rows are expected to wait. |
367	| Rows in `needs_review` | **> 0 for 1 h.** Data Salesforce rejected and we can't send. It waits on a person (Q9). |
