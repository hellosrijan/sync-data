610	<section class="section reveal" id="background-and-scenario"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-buildings" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">Background and scenario</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="body-3 measure">A client runs its sales team in Salesforce, a customer relationship management (CRM) product. Tequity has built the client an operations portal. Finance and customer-success staff work in the portal.</p><p class="body-3 measure">The same people exist in both places, as customers and as contacts. A sales person creates a customer in Salesforce. The portal should show that customer. A finance person on the portal then corrects the billing city. Salesforce should show the new city. Later, a sales person changes the phone number in Salesforce. The portal should show the new phone, and the city the finance person set should still be there.</p><p class="body-3 measure">The client also needs the history. They need to know who changed the phone, and who changed the city. The fact of an earlier edit stays on record after a later edit.</p><p class="body-3 measure">Salesforce stores its own copy. The portal stores its copy in PostgreSQL (Postgres). The two copies have to stay in sync. We are designing and building our side. Salesforce already exists, and it stays as it is.</p></div></div></section>
611	<section class="section reveal" id="the-systems-involved"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-share-network" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">The systems involved</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="body-3 measure">Three parties matter.</p><ol class="cards cols-3 wide"><li><div class="body-3"><strong>Salesforce.</strong> The client's CRM. Sales users edit records here. Salesforce has its own database.</div></li><li><div class="body-3"><strong>The portal.</strong> Our application. Finance and customer-success users edit records here. Our database is Postgres.</div></li><li><div class="body-3"><strong>The network between them.</strong> Salesforce and the portal talk over the public internet, using Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure (HTTPS). Each side keeps its own database. HTTPS is the only path between them.</div></li></ol><p class="title-3 measure">Who calls whom:</p><ul class="list list-ul measure"><li><div>When a record changes in Salesforce, Salesforce calls us. The call is a webhook: an HTTPS request to an endpoint we host. The webhook names the record and describes the change.</div></li><li><div>When a record changes in the portal, we call Salesforce. The call uses Salesforce's official record application programming interface (API): the create and update operations Salesforce already documents. The portal reaches Salesforce through that record API.</div></li><li><div>A sales user talks only to Salesforce. A portal user talks only to the portal. Neither user talks to the other system.</div></li></ul><p class="body-3 measure">The rate limit below applies to the requests we send to Salesforce. Webhooks Salesforce sends to us sit outside that cap.</p><div class="frame frame-diagram measure"><pre><code> Sales user                         Finance / customer-success user
612	     |                                          |
613	     | uses                                     | uses
614	     v                                          v
615	 Salesforce CRM                           Tequity portal

A call to the Salesforce record API is a request we initiate. Salesforce may accept it, reject it, or be unreachable.</p></div></div></section>
630	<section class="section reveal" id="requirements-and-scale"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-list-checks" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">Requirements and scale</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="title-3 measure">Records:</p><ul class="list list-ul measure"><li><div>Customers and contacts. About these fields, and no others, for this exercise: customer name, status, and owner; contact full name, phone, email, city, owner, and the customer they belong to.</div></li><li><div><strong>5 million records</strong> in production, customers and contacts counted together.</div></li></ul><p class="title-3 measure">Sync:</p><ul class="list list-ul measure"><li><div>While both systems are up, a create or an update on either side is visible on the other side within <strong>30 seconds</strong>. Thirty seconds is the worst case. Everyday sync should land sooner.</div></li><li><div>Both sides may create. Both sides may update.</div></li><li><div>The current value of a field is the one we can justify. The earlier values, who made them, and when, stay on record. That is our cap on calls to Salesforce. We choose how far under the cap to run.</p><p class="body-3 measure">We left the peak edit rate open. State the rate you assume, in edits per second, and use it in the arithmetic.</p><p class="body-3 measure">Sample webhook. This is enough to start. You may add fields. 647	<section class="section reveal" id="constraints"><div class="wrap"><header class="section-head"><div class="kicker"><i class="ph ph-barricade" aria-hidden="true"></i></div><h2 class="heading-2">Constraints</h2></header><div class="section-body"><p class="body-3 measure"><strong>Rate limit.</strong> At most 100 requests per second to the Salesforce record API. The cap counts the calls we make, including retries. Webhooks arriving at our endpoint sit outside this cap. If Salesforce tells us to slow down, we slow down, and we keep the work.</p><p class="body-3 measure"><strong>Outages.</strong> Design for the Salesforce API being unreachable for <strong>6 hours</strong>. Webhooks Salesforce tried to send during that window may never arrive. Pending calls to Salesforce must still be there after our own process restarts.</p><p class="body-3 measure">Thirty seconds applies while both systems are up. Catch-up after an outage is a different target. Drain the backlog as fast as the rate limit allows. Compute how long that takes, in minutes, for the edit rate you assumed. Show the division.</p><p class="body-3 measure"><strong>Consistency.</strong> Two people may edit the same record at the same time, on the same side or on opposite sides. Edits to different fields of that record both have to survive. Edits to the same field need one current value and a kept history.</p><p class="body-3 measure">Say what you use to decide which edit is current, on a webhook and on a portal save. Name the field. If you need an API this note does not give you, mark it as an assumption.</p><p class="body-3 measure"><strong>Out of scope for this weekend:</strong> deletes, Salesforce login screens, a visual user interface, a second region, and a load test against real Salesforce. If you write a program, call a fake CRM you write yourself.</p></div></div></section>

45	def apply(change: Change) -> Result:
46	    with transaction.atomic():
47	        try:
48	            # Savepoint, so a duplicate's IntegrityError doesn't poison the outer transaction.
49	            with transaction.atomic():
50	                ChangeEvent.objects.create(event_id=change.event_id, source=change.source)
51	        except IntegrityError:
52	            return Result(outcome="duplicate")

54	        record, created = _lock_record(change)

56	        if _is_echo(change):
57	            for name, value in change.fields.items():
58	                _write_history(change, record, name, value, FieldChange.Status.ECHO)
59	                if _is_newer(change, record, name):  # don't let a late echo overwrite a newer real value
60	                    meta = record.field_meta.get(name, {})
61	                    meta["sf_value"] = value
62	                    record.field_meta[name] = meta
63	            record.save(update_fields=["field_meta"])
64	            return Result(outcome="echo")
65	
66	        applied_fields = []
67	        for name, value in _changed_fields(change, record).items():
68	            is_newer = _is_newer(change, record, name)
69	            status = FieldChange.Status.APPLIED if is_newer else FieldChange.Status.SUPERSEDED
70	            _write_history(change, record, name, value, status)
71	            if is_newer:

75	        if created:

77	        if applied_fields:

Built (phases 2 and 3, ledgers T-003 and T-004): `WebhookView` at `/api/v1/webhooks/salesforce/`,
`WebhookEventSerializer`, token authentication (`IsSalesforceIntegration`), customer linkage (Contact `customer_salesforce_id`
and Customer arrival), step 4 inside `apply()`'s record lock, step 5 `apply()` itself, and HTTP 200 response. Brief: "Our endpoint has to accept it, check it, and apply it before anything is
stored." Webhooks can arrive twice and out of order, and `occurredAt` is Salesforce's time for
the event.

Decided. Steps 1 to 4 run before anything is stored. Step 5 is the first write.

1. **Accept:** HTTPS `POST` to the webhook endpoint. The URL is `/api/v1/webhooks/salesforce/` (`openapi.yml`).
2. **Authenticate (Q11):** DRF `TokenAuthentication`. A missing or wrong token gets **401** and
   nothing is stored. The token belongs to a dedicated integration user, and a permission check
   limits that user to this endpoint.
3. **Validate:** `WebhookEventSerializer` validates required fields: `eventId` (max 128),
   `recordId` (max 18), `objectType` (enum: `Customer`, `Contact`), `occurredAt` (ISO 8601),
   `changedBy` (max 255), and `changedFields` (dict). Fields outside the brief's scope are ignored.
   A malformed payload or missing required field gets **400** and nothing is stored.
4. **Work out what changed (Q2):** the webhook carries only the changed fields (assumption,
   matches the brief's sample). A field counts as changed only if its value differs from the
   last value we synced from Salesforce — the same comparison that makes the rule correct even
   if a webhook ever carries the whole record instead.
   - **Customer linkage for Contact:** if a Contact webhook carries a `customer` Salesforce ID (`001...`),
     it stores `customer_salesforce_id`. If that Customer already exists in Postgres, `contact.customer`
     is linked; if the Customer has not arrived yet, `contact.customer` is set to `None`. When that
     Customer arrives later via webhook or `apply()`, waiting contacts with matching `customer_salesforce_id`
     are linked automatically.
   - **Data sanitization:** optional non-nullable CharFields with `None` values (e.g. cleared strings)
     are sanitized to empty string `""` before saving.
5. **Apply:** `apply()` runs the duplicate, echo, ordering and history rules (§7 to §9) in one
   transaction.
6. **Respond 200** with `WebhookResult` schema (`{"eventId": ..., "result": outcome, "appliedFields": [...]}`)
   after the commit, also for duplicates (`result="duplicate"`), echoes (`result="echo"`), superseded
   changes (`result="superseded"`), or no-op re-deliveries (`result="no_change"`). It always returns
   HTTP 200 OK (never 201), so Salesforce stops redelivering.

The endpoint runs in the Django web process, separate from the worker (Q6).
